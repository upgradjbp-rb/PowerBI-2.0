    Module 1: Time Intelligence 00:00 – 00:06

    Day 04 Kickoff: Moving From "What Happened?" to "Which Way Are We Moving?"

    A single number like "₹28.26 Cr total revenue" tells the board nothing about momentum. Executives think in MoM (Month-over-Month), YoY (Year-over-Year), and Fiscal YTD.

    Commerce Analogy: The Balance Sheet vs. Comparative Income Statement

    In accounting, you never present FY26 revenue in isolation—you put FY25 right next to FY26 and compute % Growth. DAX Time Intelligence functions automatically shift your dim_date filter backward by 1 month, 1 quarter, or 1 full year without requiring complex VLOOKUPs!

    The 4 Mandatory Rules Before Writing Any Time Intelligence DAX
    1. You MUST have a dedicated dim_date table covering full unbroken years (2023-04-01 to 2026-03-31).
    2. dim_date MUST be officially marked via Table Tools → Mark as Date Table.
    3. Every visual's X-axis or Slicer MUST use dim_date[Date] (or its Month/Year columns), never the raw date column from fact_revenue_monthly.
    4. For Indian companies (April–March fiscal year), pass "3-31" as the year-end argument in YTD functions!
    Topic 1: Period Shifts 00:06 – 00:14

    Topic 1: `PREVIOUSMONTH()` & `DATEADD()` — Tracking Monthly Momentum

    Remember CMO Rashmi's Morning Dashboard? Every KPI card shows a "Trend MoM" badge (`-2.1%`, `+180 bps`). Here is the exact DAX behind it.

    // Step 1: Shift current month's filter back by 1 month:
    Prev Month Revenue = CALCULATE([Total Revenue INR], PREVIOUSMONTH(dim_date[Date]))

    // Step 2: Compute MoM % Change safely with DIVIDE:
    Revenue MoM % = DIVIDE([Total Revenue INR] - [Prev Month Revenue], [Prev Month Revenue], 0)
    `PREVIOUSMONTH` vs. `DATEADD`

    PREVIOUSMONTH(dim_date[Date]) always jumps back exactly 1 calendar month. If you want to jump back 3 months or 6 months (e.g., to compare against 6 months ago before the PL015 promo expired!), use DATEADD(dim_date[Date], -6, MONTH).

    Understanding Basis Points (`bps`) in Telecom & Finance

    When a percentage changes from 10.0% to 11.8%, analysts don't say "+1.8%" (which sounds tiny)—they say +180 bps (1% = 100 basis points).

    Churn MoM bps = ([Churn Rate %] - [Prev Month Churn %]) * 10000
    Topic 2: Year-over-Year 00:14 – 00:22

    Topic 2: `SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR()` — Eliminating Seasonal Noise

    Telecom usage spikes during IPL cricket season (Apr–May) and Diwali (Oct–Nov). Comparing November to October is misleading; comparing Nov 2025 to Nov 2024 is apples-to-apples!

    // Syntax: SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR( <Calendar_Date_Column> )

    SPLY Revenue = CALCULATE([Total Revenue INR], SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR(dim_date[Date]))

    Revenue YoY % = DIVIDE([Total Revenue INR] - [SPLY Revenue], [SPLY Revenue], 0)

    Topic 4: Smoothing Trends 00:30 – 00:38

    Topic 4: `DATESINPERIOD()` — Smoothing Noisy Monthly Curves

    Monthly overage charges bounce up and down every month. A 3-Month Rolling Average reveals the true underlying direction.

    // Syntax: DATESINPERIOD( <Dates>, <StartDate>, <NumberOfIntervals>, <Interval> )

    Rolling 3M Revenue =
    CALCULATE(
      [Total Revenue INR],
      DATESINPERIOD(dim_date[Date], MAX(dim_date[Date]), -3, MONTH)
    )

    How it reads: "Starting from the last date in the current month (MAX(dim_date[Date])), step backward -3 months and sum everything in that 90-day window!"

    Topic 6: Ranking & Pareto 00:46 – 00:56

    Topic 6: `RANKX()` — Ranking All 279 Cell Towers & 24 Tariff Plans

    Remember CNO Vikram Tandon's question (Q3)? "Rank all 279 towers by traffic GB and find what share the top 20% carry!" And Q4: "Rank all 24 plans by revenue contribution!"

    // Syntax: RANKX( <All_Table_Scope>, <Measure_to_Rank_By>, , DESC, Dense )

    Total Tower Traffic GB = SUM(fact_tower_metrics[traffic_gb])

    Tower Traffic Rank =
    IF(
      HASONEVALUE(dim_towers[tower_id]),
      RANKX(ALL(dim_towers[tower_id]), [Total Tower Traffic GB], , DESC, Dense)
    )
    Why `ALL(dim_towers[tower_id])` Is Mandatory Inside `RANKX`

    Inside a table visual showing one tower per row, Filter Context narrows dim_towers down to just 1 tower! If you rank a tower against a list of 1 tower, every single tower gets Rank #1! Wrapping dim_towers[tower_id] in ALL() restores the full list of all 279 towers so Power BI can assign Ranks 1 through 279.

    Topic 7: Concentration Analysis 00:56 – 01:06

    Topic 7: `TOPN()` — Computing Top-Decile Tower Traffic Share

    20% of 279 towers equals the top 56 towers. Let's write a single DAX measure that calculates what % of JioWave's entire data traffic flows through those top 56 towers!

    Top 20% Towers Traffic Share =
    VAR TopNCount = ROUND(0.20 * COUNTROWS(ALL(dim_towers)), 0) -- 56 Towers
    VAR Top56Traffic =
      CALCULATE(
        [Total Tower Traffic GB],
        TOPN(TopNCount, ALL(dim_towers), [Total Tower Traffic GB], DESC)
      )
    VAR AllTraffic = CALCULATE([Total Tower Traffic GB], ALL(dim_towers))
    RETURN
      DIVIDE(Top56Traffic, AllTraffic, 0) -- Evaluates to 46.45% of all network traffic!
